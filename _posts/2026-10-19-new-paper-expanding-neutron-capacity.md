Our paper, [*Expanding Neutron Capacity at the ESS through Symbiotic Instruments: SLEIPNIR & Remora*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06160), is now available on arXiv!

This study looks into whether a second instrument could make productive use of neutrons that would otherwise go unused, both spectrally, through the symbiotic instrument concept Remora, and spatially, through the use of vertically translated instruments like SLEIPNIR. Simulations done in McStas, a Monte Carlo ray-tracing program, show that both can reach performance comparable to established reference instruments at the ILL, despite geometric and technical constraints.

For REMORA, the focus is on extracting a wavelength band from an existing guide using a graphite monochromator upstream of the host instrument's chopper system. McStas simulations were used to optimize the layout and explore the trade-off between sample flux and energy resolution. It is shown that the concept can provide producive time-of-flight spectrometers, and highlights how the focusing-optics position determine the balance between intensity and resolution.

The simulations also underline that symbiotic instruments must be designed around their host: the available wavelength bandwidth, guide phase space and chopper configuration all affect the performance of the secondary instrument and its impact on the primary beamline.
